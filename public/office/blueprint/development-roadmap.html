🧭 Development Roadmap: Ages 6-18

6→18

DEVELOPMENT
ROADMAP

A Year-by-Year Compass for Raising
a Self-Directed, Capable Human

Mawun Valley Farm • Long-Term Vision

🧭 How to Use This Roadmap

This document is your long-term compass—not a rigid curriculum. Each year builds on the previous, creating a complete human by age 18.

"The goal is not to create a successful adult. The goal is to create an adult who can define and create their own success."

📊 The Four Phases

Phase 1: Foundation (6-8)
Building core habits, basic skills, identity formation
Phase 2: Expansion (9-11)
Increasing independence, deeper skills, first projects
Phase 3: Transition (12-14)
Abstract thinking, real responsibility, identity testing
Phase 4: Launch (15-18)
Adult-level decisions, real business, full ownership

🎯 Core Threads (Every Year)

  • Producer Mindset — Creating, not just consuming
  • System Thinking — Understanding how things work
  • Emotional Grounding — Self-awareness and regulation
  • Real Consequences — Learning from reality, not lectures
  • Increasing Autonomy — More freedom = more responsibility

Key Principle: Each year, slightly increase difficulty, responsibility, and freedom—together.

1

PHASE 1: FOUNDATION

Ages 6-8 • Building the Base

These three years establish the operating system that everything else runs on. Focus on habits, not achievements.

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

  • Establish daily learning rhythm
  • Build identity as "someone who figures things out"
  • Create comfort with productive struggle
  • Develop basic numeracy and literacy
  • Learn to follow through on commitments

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making learning feel like punishment
  • Over-praising ("You're so smart!") vs. effort praise
  • Removing all struggle to keep them happy
  • Inconsistency in daily structure

✅ Success Indicators (End of Phase 1)

  • Can sit and focus for 30+ minutes
  • Reads simple books independently
  • Understands basic money concepts
  • Takes responsibility for small tasks without reminders
  • Comfortable talking to adults (guests, workers)
2
AGE 6

The Foundations Year

Core theme: Establishing rhythm and basic habits

🧠 Cognitive Development

  • Concrete thinking dominates (can't think abstractly yet)
  • Learns best through doing, not explaining
  • Short attention span (15-20 min max per activity)
  • Beginning to understand cause and effect

📚 Learning Focus

Math

Counting to 100, basic addition, grouping objects, real money handling

Reading

Letter sounds, simple words, picture books, 10-15 min daily

Writing

Letter formation, name writing, simple 3-word sentences

Life Skills

Dressing self, basic hygiene, simple chores, animal care basics

🌿 Farm Integration

  • Feeding animals (with supervision)
  • Collecting eggs daily
  • Watering specific plants
  • Greeting guests ("Hello, welcome!")

💰 Micro-Economy Start

First "Job": Egg collector with small weekly allowance tied to completion. Track on simple chart. Introduce concept of saving vs. spending.
3
AGE 7

The Systems Year

Core theme: Understanding how things connect and work

🧠 Cognitive Development

  • Can follow multi-step instructions
  • Begins understanding sequences and patterns
  • Longer focus periods (25-30 min possible)
  • Asks "why" questions constantly — encourage this

📚 Learning Focus

Math

Addition/subtraction to 20, intro multiplication (grouping), telling time, measuring

Reading

Simple chapter books, reading aloud, comprehension questions

Writing

Daily journal (3-5 sentences), describing observations

Systems

How farm produces food, how café makes money, guest cycle

🌿 Farm Integration (Expanded)

  • Owns specific animals (responsible for their wellbeing)
  • Helps prepare simple items for café
  • Tracks daily egg count in a notebook
  • Gives mini "farm tours" to guest children

🧗 Challenge Upgrade

First Sale: Sell something he produces (eggs, juice, crafts) to real guests. Handle money, make change, experience profit/loss.

⚠️ Watch For

Comparison with other kids (especially if they're in school). Reinforce that his path is intentionally different, not "less than."
4
AGE 8

The Ownership Year

Core theme: Taking real responsibility with real consequences

🧠 Cognitive Development

  • Can plan ahead (today affects tomorrow)
  • Understands fairness and rules more deeply
  • Developing personal interests and preferences
  • Can work independently for 30-45 minutes

📚 Learning Focus

Math

Multiplication tables (2,5,10), division intro, money calculations, basic fractions

Reading

Chapter books, non-fiction interests, 30+ min reading sessions

Writing

Paragraphs, simple reports, tracking business income/expense

Projects

First long-term project (4+ weeks), building something real

💼 Business Expansion

Real Micro-Business: He should have a defined "business" by now—eggs, farm tours, juice stand—with:
  • Simple profit tracking
  • Pricing decisions (his choice)
  • Customer interaction
  • Saving goal (something he wants to buy)

🔨 Skills Introduction

  • Building: Use real tools (hammer, saw with supervision)
  • Digital: Take photos/videos, basic editing
  • Cooking: Simple recipes start to finish

🎯 End of Phase 1 Milestone

By age 8, he should be able to manage a full day's routine with minimal prompting, run a small business independently, and handle basic conflicts without adult intervention.
5

PHASE 2: EXPANSION

Ages 9-11 • Building Real Capability

The foundation is set. Now we expand capability and independence. These years determine whether he becomes a doer or a dreamer.

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

  • Develop genuine expertise in 2-3 areas
  • Handle multi-day projects independently
  • Manage real money with real consequences
  • Build relationships beyond family
  • Develop critical thinking (question everything)

🔄 Key Shifts in Phase 2

From → To

Guided tasks → Self-initiated projects

From → To

Playing business → Real business

From → To

Learning for parents → Learning for self

From → To

Local awareness → World awareness

⚠️ Critical Warning

Ages 9-11 are when children often "check out" mentally if not challenged enough. Signs: boredom, resistance, doing minimum effort. Fix: increase difficulty AND autonomy together.

✅ Success Indicators (End of Phase 2)

  • Has completed at least one significant project (weeks of work)
  • Manages own schedule with weekly check-ins only
  • Has earned and saved meaningful money
  • Can explain complex systems (business, nature, society)
  • Has mentors/relationships outside family
6
AGE 9

The Independence Year

Core theme: Self-direction with accountability

🧠 Cognitive Development

  • Can think ahead weeks, not just days
  • Understands abstract concepts better
  • Strong sense of fairness and justice
  • Peer relationships become very important

📚 Learning Evolution

Math

All operations, percentages (for business), area/perimeter, data tracking

Reading

Diverse genres, first "hard" books, reading for research

Writing

Business plans (simple), persuasive writing, emails

Research

Finding information independently, evaluating sources

💼 Business Development

Business Upgrade: Scale the micro-business OR start a new one. Goals:
  • Track weekly profit/loss
  • Set and work toward quarterly savings goal
  • Make one significant purchase with own money

🌍 Expanded World

  • Travel: Trips to other parts of Indonesia (observe different systems)
  • People: Regular interaction with skilled adults (apprenticeship light)
  • Ideas: Introduce big questions: "Why are some countries poor?"

🔧 Skills Deep Dive

Pick ONE area for deeper focus this year:

  • Building/Construction (help with real farm projects)
  • Agriculture (own garden plot, full cycle)
  • Digital Creation (YouTube channel about farm life)
  • Hospitality (real role in guest experience)
7
AGE 10

The Projects Year

Core theme: Completing meaningful work over extended time

🧠 Cognitive Development

  • Can handle complex, multi-step projects
  • Beginning to think about future self
  • Strong opinions forming — encourage debate
  • Can understand multiple perspectives

🏗️ The Big Project

One Major Project (3-6 months):

Examples for your context:

  • Build a chicken coop from scratch
  • Create and run a "kids farm camp" for guest children
  • Start a YouTube/social media about farm life
  • Design and plant a new garden section

He chooses. You advise. He owns outcome.

📚 Learning Integration

Learning should now be project-driven, not subject-driven:

  • Need math? Calculate materials for the coop
  • Need writing? Create promotional content
  • Need research? Figure out best chicken breeds

💰 Financial Milestone

First "Real" Money Goal: Something meaningful (electronics, trip, equipment) that requires months of saving. Track progress visually. Experience delayed gratification.

🤝 Mentorship

Identify 2-3 adults outside family who can teach specific skills:

  • Local builder for construction
  • Farmer for agriculture
  • Business owner for business thinking
8
AGE 11

The Pre-Launch Year

Core theme: Preparing for the adolescent transition

🧠 Cognitive Development

  • Abstract thinking emerging strongly
  • Questions authority (healthy, don't suppress)
  • Identity exploration begins
  • Capable of genuine expertise in areas of interest

⚠️ Critical Preparation

Puberty is coming (12-14). This year, establish:
  • Open communication about body changes, emotions
  • Strong identity foundation ("I am someone who...")
  • Clear values that he's internalized, not just obeying
  • Trusted relationships beyond parents

💼 Business Maturity

End of Year Goal:
  • Running business requires less than 2 hours/week of parental input
  • Has made at least one significant "mistake" and recovered
  • Understands profit margins, customer acquisition, value creation

🌍 Worldview Expansion

  • Economics: Why do prices change? What is inflation?
  • History: How did Indonesia develop? What patterns repeat?
  • Philosophy: What makes a good life? (Simple discussions)

🎯 End of Phase 2 Milestone

By 11, he should have: completed a major project, managed real money, developed expertise in at least one area, and be able to hold conversations with adults as a near-equal in his domains.
9

PHASE 3: TRANSITION

Ages 12-14 • Navigating Adolescence

The most challenging phase. Body changes, identity questions, push against authority. Your job: stay connected while letting go.

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

  • Navigate physical/emotional changes healthily
  • Develop personal values (not just inherited ones)
  • Handle real failure and real success
  • Build identity independent of parents
  • Maintain growth mindset through challenges

🔄 Your Role Changes

From → To

Director → Advisor

From → To

Deciding for him → Guiding his decisions

From → To

Protecting → Preparing

From → To

Daily structure → Weekly check-ins

⚠️ The Adolescent Challenge

Expect resistance, mood swings, and identity testing. This is normal and necessary. The worst response is becoming authoritarian. The best response: firm boundaries + emotional availability.

✅ Success Indicators (End of Phase 3)

  • Has weathered at least one major failure
  • Can articulate personal values and defend them
  • Maintains meaningful pursuits despite emotional turbulence
  • Has healthy peer relationships
  • Communicates openly about struggles
10
AGE 12

The Identity Year

Core theme: "Who am I becoming?"

🧠 What's Happening

  • Puberty typically begins (physical changes)
  • Emotional intensity increases dramatically
  • Peer opinion matters more than ever
  • May push back against everything
  • Abstract thinking fully online

👨‍👩‍👦 Parenting Adjustments

Key shift: More questions, fewer instructions.
  • "What do you think you should do?"
  • "What would happen if...?"
  • "I trust you to figure this out"

💼 Work Evolution

  • Business should be genuinely profitable (not play money)
  • Consider: real job/internship outside family business
  • Exposure to different work environments
  • Understanding of employment vs. entrepreneurship

📚 Learning Structure

Move toward self-directed learning:

  • He sets weekly learning goals
  • You review together on weekends
  • Focus on depth in chosen areas, breadth elsewhere
  • Online courses, books, mentors — his choice

🗣️ Conversations to Have

  • Body changes and sexuality (matter-of-fact, open)
  • Peer pressure and decision-making
  • Digital life and social media
  • What kind of person do you want to be?
11
AGE 13

The Testing Year

Core theme: Testing boundaries, values, and identity

⚠️ Expect Turbulence

13 is often the most challenging year. He may:
  • Reject things he previously loved
  • Be moody, withdrawn, or confrontational
  • Question everything you've taught
  • Seem to "regress" in maturity
This is normal. Stay calm. Stay connected.

🎯 Your Focus

  • Connection over control — Don't win battles, win the relationship
  • Natural consequences — Let reality teach when possible
  • Firm boundaries — Clear lines (safety, respect) that don't move
  • Emotional availability — "I'm here when you want to talk"

💼 Work/Purpose

Keep him engaged in meaningful work. Idle teenagers struggle most. Ensure:
  • Real responsibilities that matter
  • Clear contribution to family enterprise
  • His own projects that he owns

🧭 Values Conversations

Don't lecture. Ask questions that make him think:

  • "What kind of friend do you want to be?"
  • "What would you do if everyone else was doing X?"
  • "What matters more than money?"
  • "How do you want to be remembered?"

🌍 Expanded Freedom

With demonstrated responsibility, increase autonomy:

  • Solo trips to town
  • Managing own schedule for days at a time
  • Larger financial decisions
12
AGE 14

The Emergence Year

Core theme: The real person begins to emerge from adolescent chaos

🧠 What's Shifting

  • Worst turbulence often passing
  • More capable of nuanced thinking
  • Beginning to appreciate parents again (slightly!)
  • Future orientation increases
  • Real passions becoming clear

🎯 Focus Areas

Deep Expertise

Double down on his strongest area. This becomes competitive advantage.

Real World Exposure

Internships, apprenticeships, work experience outside family.

Leadership

Put him in charge of something with real stakes.

Network

Help him build relationships with successful adults.

💼 Business/Work Milestone

By end of 14:
  • Has generated at least $500+ in personal revenue
  • Can run a project with minimal supervision
  • Has failed at something significant and recovered
  • Understands basic business concepts (revenue, cost, profit, value)

📚 Academic Considerations

If formal qualifications matter for his path:

  • Begin preparing for any needed certifications/exams
  • Identify gaps in formal knowledge
  • Consider selective formal courses if needed

🎯 End of Phase 3 Milestone

By 14: Survived adolescent transition with relationship intact. Has clear interests/passions. Demonstrates responsibility. Ready for adult-level challenges.
13

PHASE 4: LAUNCH

Ages 15-18 • Becoming an Adult

The final phase. Your job is to make yourself unnecessary. By 18, he should be able to live independently — even if he chooses not to.

🎯 Phase 4 Goals

  • Full ownership of life decisions
  • Genuine expertise in chosen fields
  • Financial capability (can support self if needed)
  • Clear direction (even if it changes later)
  • Healthy independence from parents

🔄 Final Role Transition

You Become

Consultant (when asked), supporter, safety net

He Becomes

Primary decision-maker for his own life

💡 Key Principle

Treat him as an adult who hasn't moved out yet, not a child who's getting older.

✅ Success Indicators (Age 18)

  • Could live independently (has the skills)
  • Has meaningful work or clear path
  • Manages own finances responsibly
  • Makes major decisions independently
  • Has support network beyond family
  • Maintains good relationship with parents by choice
14
AGE 15

The Specialization Year

Core theme: Going deep, not wide

🎯 Strategic Focus

By 15, it's time to specialize. Jack-of-all-trades was fine before. Now pick 1-2 areas to become genuinely excellent.

The 1000-Hour Question: What is he willing to invest 1000+ hours in over the next 3 years? This becomes his competitive advantage.
  • Agriculture/permaculture
  • Hospitality/tourism
  • Digital creation/marketing
  • Construction/building
  • Business/entrepreneurship
  • Something else entirely

💼 Real Work

  • Significant role in family business (not "helping"—owning outcomes)
  • Or: real job/internship outside family
  • Or: his own business at meaningful scale

💰 Financial Independence Practice

Money Goals:
  • Covers own discretionary spending
  • Contributes to larger purchases (phone, travel)
  • Has savings account with growing balance
  • Understands investing basics

🌍 Expanded World

  • Solo travel experience (within Indonesia)
  • Exposure to different life paths
  • Mentors in his area of focus
  • Online communities in his interests
15
AGE 16-17

The Capability Years

Core theme: Building adult-level capability

🎯 By End of 17, He Should Be Able To:

Life Skills

Cook, clean, manage household, handle emergencies, navigate bureaucracy

Financial

Budget, save, invest basics, understand taxes, negotiate

Social

Network professionally, resolve conflicts, lead small teams

Professional

Create value that people pay for, deliver consistently

💼 Work at Scale

Real Contribution: By 16-17, he should be making a real, measurable contribution to family enterprise OR running his own thing at real scale. Not "learning" — doing.

📚 Learning Path

Three options to consider (not mutually exclusive):

  • Direct work: Full focus on business/skill development
  • Alternative credentials: Online courses, certifications, portfolios
  • Traditional path: University preparation if relevant for his goals

Key: He chooses. You advise.

🗺️ Future Planning

Help him think through:

  • Where do you want to be at 25?
  • What paths could get you there?
  • What do you need to learn/do/become?
  • What are you optimizing for? (Money? Freedom? Impact? Location?)

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Pushing him toward "safe" paths that aren't his
  • Under-challenging (too much comfort)
  • Over-managing his decisions
  • Comparing to peers on traditional paths
16
AGE 18

The Launch Year

Core theme: Ready to fly — whether he stays or goes

🎯 The Goal

At 18, he should be CAPABLE of full independence — even if he chooses to stay connected to family business/home.

✅ Readiness Checklist

Can He...

  • ☐ Support himself financially if needed?
  • ☐ Manage a household (food, bills, maintenance)?
  • ☐ Navigate adult systems (banking, legal, health)?
  • ☐ Make major decisions and live with consequences?
  • ☐ Build and maintain healthy relationships?
  • ☐ Handle failure, rejection, and setbacks?
  • ☐ Continue learning without external structure?
  • ☐ Create value that others will pay for?

🔄 Relationship Evolution

The parent-child relationship should now become adult-to-adult:

  • He asks for advice (sometimes), you give it (when asked)
  • Major decisions are his to make
  • Connection is by choice, not dependency
  • Mutual respect as capable adults

🌟 What Success Looks Like

Success is not a specific outcome (college, business, career). Success is a human who can enter any system, build within it, or create his own — and most importantly, live consciously, fully, and on his own terms.
17

🧭 Final Wisdom: The Long Game

What Really Matters

"The goal of parenting is not to raise a successful child. It's to raise an adult who no longer needs you — and still wants you in their life."

Remember Along the Way

  • Progress isn't linear. Some years will feel like steps backward. They're not.
  • Your relationship matters more than any curriculum. A connected child learns. A disconnected one resists.
  • He's watching you more than listening to you. Model what you want him to become.
  • Struggle is not damage. Appropriate challenge builds resilience.
  • You'll make mistakes. Repair matters more than perfection.

The North Star (Every Decision)

"Is this making him more capable, more aware, and more independent?"

If yes → continue. If no → adjust.

You've Already Won

Most parents never think this deeply. Most children never get this opportunity. By creating this roadmap and committing to it, you've already set him apart.

The rest is showing up, year after year, with love and intention.
